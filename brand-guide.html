Fundraising UI: design tokens & brand guide

Taken from the public stylesheets of gofundme.com (en-GB homepage, consumer light theme, November 2026). The site's internal design system uses the hrt- prefix. Use this guide to study the system and as a base for your own design, not to reproduce GoFundMe's brand.

Brand principles

The 2026 identity (by the studio Koto) is built around the idea that help adds up: small individual acts that build shared momentum. Three principles guide the art direction: humanity, community and optimism.

Green is the anchor

A deep forest green and a bright lime-green highlight carry the brand. Supporting hues (teal, yellow, purple, pink) appear as soft two-tone pairs: a light background with a dark text colour of the same hue.

Progress as a symbol

The progress ring and goal bar are the brand's key graphic. A gradient running from lime to green shows momentum, and the arc shape repeats in section edges and image crops.

Warm, round, legible

One rounded geometric typeface, pill-shaped buttons, generous radii, very soft shadows and lots of white space.

Primitive palette

These are every raw colour value found in the system, arranged into scales. In Figma, they become your Primitives variable collection. Click a swatch to copy its hex value.

Semantic colour roles

Components should only use these role names, never the raw palette. Names follow the system's pattern category / intent / emphasis: subtle is the lightest, then medium, then strong, and on-strong means content placed on top of a strong colour.

Every role has a full set of states, such as -hover, -pressed and -on-strong. The complete list of about 400 roles is in tokens.json and tokens.css.

Typography

The site uses a single variable typeface, GoFundMe Sans, a custom font that isn't licensed for outside use. Its fallbacks are Trebuchet, Helvetica and Arial. This guide uses Figtree (free on Google Fonts) as a close substitute. The font has three weights; its custom weight axis maps to standard CSS weights as follows: 100 → Regular (400), 130 → Medium (500), 175 → Bold (700). Font sizes scale smoothly between a 375px and a 1440px screen width.

Rules

  • Display and heading styles: Bold, line height 1.1, tight tracking (−2% for display sizes, −1% for heading-xl to heading-md).
  • Body text: Regular, line height 1.5. Body-sm uses a fixed 20px line height. Buttons and emphasised body text use Bold.
  • Legend (20px Regular, line height 1.1) is for large figures and supporting statements, such as the hero statistic.
  • Headings use the default text colour (#232323). On dark-green surfaces, use the lime-green highlight (#CCF88E).

Spacing, grid & breakpoints

The spacing scale is built on 8px steps, with ¼, ½ and 1½ steps added for fine adjustments.

Breakpoints & containers

NameBreakpointContainer width

Containers have 24px side padding. Their maximum width grows from 768px (md) to 1024px (lg) to 1152px (xl), and content tops out at 1200px. Homepage sections use large vertical padding: 88–112px on mobile and 120–168px on desktop.

Shadows (always soft and neutral)

Use shadows sparingly: for popovers, sticky bars and raised cards. Most surfaces separate from the background using a fill colour (#FAFAFA or #F5F5F5) or a 1px #D8D8D8 border instead of a shadow.

Core components

Buttons

All buttons are pill-shaped and come in three heights: XL 56px, MD 40px and SM 32px. Side padding is 24px for XL and MD, and 16px for SM. Labels are Bold, 16px, with a 20px line height. Focus state: 2px #232323 outline, offset by 2px.

Arc section edge

Sections are separated by a wide elliptical curve: an SVG mask with a 1440×90 proportion, raised 80% above the section. Images in the hero are also cropped along the same curve.

Common patterns: fade in while rising 16px, a "bloop" scale (0.1 → 1.1 → 1), the sticky call-to-action bar sliding up, and step rings filling clockwise. Every animation turns off when the user has reduced motion enabled.

Voice & imagery

Tone: a helpful guide

The tone is reassuring, practical and direct. Copy speaks to the reader as "you", uses short sentences and leads with the benefit. Examples: "Get started in just a few minutes", "We've got you covered". The site uses British spelling and £ on en-GB pages.

Trust signals

Use concrete numbers ("100+ million people", "£35 million raised every week") with a footnote, and keep repeating the trust message: no fee to start, secure payments, a guarantee.

Photography

Show real people in candid moments of community and support, in warm natural light. Images are cropped into arcs, circles or soft-angled blocks. Avoid stock-photo posing.

Do / Don't

Do

  • Pair each light accent background with the dark text colour of the same hue
  • Keep one primary call to action per view
  • Use the progress gradient only to show momentum toward a goal
  • Leave generous section padding and keep layouts centred

Don't

  • Put lime green (#CCF88E) text on a white background, because it fails contrast
  • Mix more than two accent hues in one component
  • Use heavy or coloured drop shadows
  • Use GoFundMe's logo, sun mark, progress-circle mark or typeface in your own product

Bringing it into Figma

  1. Install the Tokens Studio for Figma plugin, go to Settings → Load from file and import tokens.json. It contains two token sets, primitives and semantic; semantic tokens point to primitive ones.
  2. Click Styles & Variables → Export to Figma. Primitives become a variable collection, semantic roles become a second collection that points to the first, and the text styles are created as a desktop and a mobile set.
  3. Install Figtree, or swap in your own typeface by changing the single font.family.figma token.
  4. Developers can use tokens.css directly. It contains the same names as CSS custom properties, including the fluid type scale.
Before you make this your own brand, change the green and the typeface. The structure (role names, scales, states) is safe to reuse. The specific mix of forest green, lime highlight and arc-shaped progress is what makes it recognisably GoFundMe.
